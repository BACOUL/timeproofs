# TIMEPROOFS V2 — G6

Assessment date: 2 October 2026. Decision: **G6 CONDITIONAL**.

TimeProofs has a useful executable reference, but a defensible independent infrastructure business is not established. The broad originality claim is contradicted by existing policy-based attestation verification, credential requirement/acquisition standards, and recent agent accountability work. A narrower opportunity remains worth one external experiment: collecting and checking a portable purchase dossier when authorization, processor execution and merchant confirmation live in different systems.

## Scope and evidence quality

Inspected BACOUL/timeproofs branch `timeproofs-v2`, starting at `efcf0e77fdfd0e5d64e8c42d44cf9e250bda10a2`: source, profiles, collectors, verifier, SDK, HTTP server, public copy, acceptance records and tests. Reran `npm run check`: 33/33 passed. The public browser demo progressed through 3/5, 4/5 and 5/5. These establish bounded technical behavior, not demand or real external acquisition.

Current comprehension fixes are on commit `4954fcf67c2a8e9bc385b0638d2b5842261693aa`, with a READY preview at https://timeproofs-khcdsx8g6-jeason1.vercel.app/. Only explanatory copy and a runnable SDK example changed. No resolver semantics, new profile, protocol adapter, billing or major feature was added.

Primary specifications, official product documentation, repositories and research papers underpin the ecosystem review. A documented capability is not necessarily a tested production capability. A draft is not an adopted standard; a paper is not a commercial deployment. “Not documented” means the inspected material did not establish the capability, not that a vendor cannot implement it. Research is current to the retrieved materials on this date, not an exhaustive proof that no competitor exists.

### Falsification result

The proposition “TimeProofs is more than a receipt format” is supported by its code. It resolves a declared checklist and dependencies rather than merely signing a record.

The proposition “this layer is uniquely absent from the ecosystem” is not supported. Witness/Archivista covers most of the complete workflow in its software domain: evidence generation/collection, retrieval, policy evaluation, missing-required-attestation failure, dependency consistency and portable evidence. in-toto already supplies the underlying declared-workflow verification pattern. Agent accountability composition drafts and the preserved-bundle research address the cross-party sufficiency concept directly. x401/DCQL already covers credential-demand and acquisition portions.

AP2 v0.2 is especially important: it already has checkout and payment receipts, their mandates, deterministic checks and dispute evidence. Its specification explicitly leaves retention/retrieval details outside scope and identifies automated checkout-mandate retrieval by transaction identifier as useful. That is a possible implementation opportunity, not proof of proprietary novelty or customer willingness to pay.

No inspected solution was verified as a production, general-purpose service that automatically acquires arbitrary MCP/A2A/AP2/SCITT artifacts, applies arbitrary action profiles and exports a fully replayable lifecycle dossier. **TimeProofs does not implement that complete workflow either.** This comparison cannot award it advantages for planned adapters.

## What TimeProofs uniquely adds

Within this reference, it packages five explicit purchase requirements, visible gap requests, prerequisite-aware collector calls, cross-artifact transaction matching and an export whose results are recomputed under separate trust roots. That is a coherent developer experience. Its potentially differentiating product contribution is reducing the integration and maintenance work required to assemble evidence from separately operated systems.

“Unique” here is a candidate product fit, not an exclusive algorithm or defensible moat. It needs to save meaningful recurring work relative to native verifiers plus a small application script. Cross-protocol interoperability, production acquisition reliability and evidence of those savings remain unproven.

## What TimeProofs does not uniquely add

Signatures, tamper detection, hash binding, signed receipts, portable bundles, deterministic rules, multiple evidence checks, dependencies, issuer trust constraints, missing credential requests and offline verification all exist elsewhere. A five-item checklist does not by itself constitute new infrastructure.

The alpha is narrower than its long-term hypothesis: profiles are code-defined and selected explicitly; it does not infer necessary requirements. Only purchase executes. JWT/VC artifacts need TimeProofs application claims such as requirement, role and exact action hash. Existing AP2 mandates cannot be passed in unchanged. This is a real normalization/issuer adoption burden even though the signature envelope is standard. HTTP resolve checks supplied evidence; asynchronous acquisition callbacks are local SDK functionality. Different issuer roles do not automatically mean different independent organizations.

SATISFIED means the declared checks pass under the explicit trust policy and evaluation time. It is not legal authority, truthful content, settlement finality, physical delivery, proof of the absence of hidden contradictory evidence, or an immutable historical verdict. The current verifier evaluates current expiry/revocation: a once-satisfied export may later fail. It needs the matching profile/engine and trusted policy, not just JSON alone.

## Receipts vs TimeProofs: authorized_purchase

Scenario: Acme's procurement agent purchases equipment for EUR 28,000. Evidence questions are delegated authority, identity, agreed offer, processor execution and merchant order confirmation. The last item is an attested confirmation, not physical delivery.

| Method | What a reviewer has | Useful checks | Remaining work / limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Logs only | Agent trace, authorization log, processor event, merchant response | Application can correlate identifiers and check amount/scope; immutable/signed logging can improve assurance | Reviewer trusts instrumentation/operator and retention; reconstructing a dossier may require several systems |
| B. One signed action receipt | Signer's claims about this purchase | Authenticity and integrity; a rich receipt can include policy references and several linked claims | Scope depends on receipt content and issuer. One receipt is sufficient if it already contains the required trusted evidence and checks; no mandatory TimeProofs advantage |
| C. AP2/VI and protocol evidence | Mandates, checkout/payment receipts, cryptographic bindings | Native deterministic authorization, constraints and cross-checks; AP2 explicitly supports bringing artifacts together for disputes | Retention/retrieval and company-specific evidence outside protocol scope require implementation. For an entirely AP2-covered transaction, TimeProofs may be redundant |
| D. Current TimeProofs profile/resolver | Five signed fixtures, checklist, gaps, linked transaction IDs and exported case | 3/5 → processor collector → 4/5 → merchant collector → 5/5; independent recomputation; absent evidence remains incomplete | No real AP2 adapter, merchant connection, settlement guarantee, source-universe commitment or production replay registry |

Executed evidence: the 33-test suite verifies missing evidence, prerequisite order and a validly signed merchant confirmation with a different processor transaction returning CONFLICT. Amount/action substitutions fail their checks. Both format fixtures contribute to the same profile. These tests establish consistency checking, not superiority over AP2/Witness.

**Material value is conditional.** If a developer already retains native signed mandates/receipts and its native verifier answers the reviewer, another resolver adds little. If the actual task is repeatedly retrieving missing independent artifacts and reconciling them across organizational boundaries, shared orchestration can save maintenance and incident-review work. That second situation must be demonstrated externally.

## Weakest use cases

Generic receipt signing, a generic “prove identity before access” gate, ordinary single-stack purchases, generic CI attestation verification and generic contract-signing proof. Existing tools naturally supply these. Fund transfer and universal deletion are poor initial choices because the integration/assurance burden overwhelms a solo validation budget.

## Adoption risks

The main risk is that a developer sees a checklist it could implement cheaply itself. Additional risks are limited native protocol support, producer-side claim mapping, unavailable independent attestations, pinned-key lifecycle work, privacy objections to hosted acquisition, dependence on matching profile/engine versions, and the cost of maintaining adapters. A managed service must justify being another runtime dependency.

The alpha has no committed source universe: an exporter can omit contradictory evidence and build another case. The Resolver detects inconsistencies in supplied artifacts, not all real-world omissions. It also cannot enforce cross-case replay prevention or actual execution from a signed assertion. These boundaries may be acceptable for a bounded dossier checker; they are incompatible with an unconditional “complete proof” promise.

## External adoption test design

One bounded experiment, no further product build: **unassisted purchase reproduction followed by integration into one existing developer-owned sandbox workflow**, compared with that developer's existing alternative.

Recruit 6–8 outside developers who currently operate agent-assisted purchasing or related commerce automation. At least two should be familiar with AP2/VI or native signed-receipt verification, to avoid a weak comparison group. Invite through opt-in developer channels; do not automatically send invitations or messages. Keep any honorarium independent of positive feedback. Use fictitious/sanitized data and retain no production secrets.

Neutral introduction:

> TimeProofs is an experimental library that checks supplied evidence against a declared action profile and exports a JSON case. The current reference uses signed purchase fixtures. We are testing whether its integration work and results improve an actual developer workflow compared with existing tools.

Preview: https://timeproofs-khcdsx8g6-jeason1.vercel.app/demo

**Single task:** Reproduce authorized_purchase from the preview and repository, complete its missing execution/outcome evidence, export the case and verify it locally. Then show how the same task would fit one existing sandbox workflow you own, using its existing evidence sources; identify what requires mapping or cannot be represented. Compare with the solution you would otherwise use. Do not change or build another profile during this test.

Give only the link, task and repository quickstart. Allow 30 minutes for unassisted reproduction, then up to a week for a voluntary sandbox integration. Record attempts, time, errors, questions, requirement misunderstandings, code changed, artifacts produced, alternative chosen and whether the developer returns without prompting. Assistance is recorded, not silently counted as unassisted success. Source fixtures must never be reported as external issuer integration.

Eight post-task questions:

1. Explain what SATISFIED establishes and what it cannot establish for this purchase.
2. Show the workflow and evidence source where you would insert this, or explain why you would not.
3. What would you use instead? Show the native verifier, code or manual procedure.
4. Which part would you remove from TimeProofs, and why?
5. Would you integrate this into a production agent? What concrete prerequisite or approval would be required first?
6. What prevented you from finishing, or cost you the most integration time?
7. Which evidence requirement/source is absent or incorrectly modeled for your workflow?
8. Who owns the budget for maintaining this workflow, and what would they pay to have someone operate this exact piece? What next action can you take to test that budget?

Strong behavioral signals: developer supplies a real sanitized requirement/artifact; writes and retains an integration; another engineer verifies its case without the operator's UI; a missing or mismatched artifact exposes a real reconciliation defect; developer returns with another case; budget owner accepts a priced pilot with specific scope. A feature request or compliment alone is not a strong signal.

Weak signals: demo completion only; hypothetical usefulness; requests for many unrelated profiles; interest only in free signing; no existing consequential workflow; no independent consumer; no retained code or second use; demand for a dashboard instead of the case/verifier.

Predeclared management criteria, not statistical proof: at least 5 of 6 first participants reproduce and explain the boundary without help; at least 2 independently proceed to their own sandbox integration; at least 1 produces a cross-source case for another engineer and returns for a second use; at least 1 relevant budget owner makes a concrete priced-pilot commitment. Failure of comprehension alone sends the experiment back to copy fixes, not automatically to product rejection.

Product hypothesis invalidation: qualified participants show their native AP2/receipt/attestation stack already answers every relevant question with less work; no recurring cross-source evidence gap is found; needed source evidence cannot be obtained; integration mostly forces issuers to adopt TimeProofs claims; no independent reviewer wants the dossier; or complete task/return-use success produces no paid operation demand. Repeated custom consulting requirements incompatible with automated solo operation invalidate the proposed hosted model. If only local SDK adoption appears, preserve the useful library but reject the independent SaaS assumption.

## Commercial model

All 0/49/199/799 EUR tiers remain hypotheses. There is no direct willingness-to-pay evidence for TimeProofs. Existing spending on observability, policy management and attestation hosting is evidence of adjacent operational budgets, not evidence that these buyers will add another service.

| Tier hypothesis | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Open — EUR 0 | Appropriate for specifications, public profiles, local resolver/verifier, SDK/CLI, examples, case export and reproducible checks. Private local use should also be allowed; secrecy alone should not trigger payment |
| Developer — EUR 49/month | Plausible experiment anchor for bounded hosted operation, not a validated market price. Could be too expensive for simple checks or too low for support-heavy acquisition |
| Pro — EUR 199/month | Needs demonstrated recurring collector operation, useful retention and profile/policy operations; higher case counts alone are a weak justification |
| Business — EUR 799/month | Requires real organizational reliability/security value. Promising this before tenant isolation, operational controls and support exist would be premature |
| Enterprise — custom | Buyer review, dedicated deployment and contractual support are expensive for a solo founder. Do not make this the initial distribution model |

Recommended candidate paid unit: an **operated evidence workflow per workspace/environment**, with included unique action resolutions, bounded collector attempts and storage/retention. Meter infrastructure costs separately and transparently. Make the bill predictable before execution.

Proof Case volume is a credible secondary metric if tied to one stable action ID and a materially bounded workload. It is a poor sole metric: one action may be re-resolved many times, incomplete cases are valuable, artifacts/retries vary, and verification is computationally inexpensive compared with network acquisition and retention. Do not charge repeatedly for every refresh, retry or external verifier. Prevent arbitrary case splitting from changing the price of the same action.

Paid value could be connector maintenance, controlled scheduled acquisition, retry/reconciliation operation, retention/search, policy lifecycle, access controls, key-management operations and documented reliability. These are possible commercial services, not implemented entitlements. Core verifiability/export must remain available without subscription so cases remain useful when service ends.

Private Proof Profiles are commercially meaningful only when their lifecycle requires versioning, testing, approval, controlled rollout and organization-specific maintenance. A private JSON checklist by itself is not a EUR 199 feature; OPA/Cedar/Cerbos already offer strong alternatives. In the current alpha, profiles cannot be loaded arbitrarily, so private-profile claims are prospective.

A hosted Resolver is **not automatically the primary product**. If users mainly run cheap local checks, hosting offers little. Managed acquisition plus retained evidence operations is the stronger hosted hypothesis. Privacy requirements may instead favor local/VPC operation. The external experiment must distinguish library adoption, managed-service willingness to pay and custom-service work.

Measure actual per-action artifacts, acquisition calls/failures/retries, storage/egress/retention, support minutes, active workflows and buyer-defined incident/review cost. Model contribution margin including external-provider charges, operational support and failures; local millisecond timings do not establish SaaS economics. No billing implementation is authorized by this gate.

## Standards risk

**High.** AP2 already incorporates linked mandates and receipts; its remaining retrieval gap is a natural SDK/provider feature. x401/DCQL owns credential demands. Capsule composition drafts and agent accountability research address explicit cross-record sufficiency. A TimeProofs-specific requirement vocabulary could become an interoperability obstacle rather than an asset. Prefer consuming native verification results and preserving originals if later external demand warrants adapters. Do not build those adapters during G6.

## Competitive risk

**High for generic infrastructure; unresolved for a narrow operated workflow.** Witness/Archivista, governance receipt services, policy platforms, payment providers and observability incumbents have distribution and existing integrations. Open-source primitives make basic checking cheap to reproduce. A credible advantage would be unusually easy integration into a recurring uncovered task and cheaper operation of its collectors, not the presence of signatures or SATISFIED.

## G6 decision and single next product experiment

**G6 CONDITIONAL**.

PASS is premature: no uniquely defensible cross-protocol capability or external adoption is demonstrated. FAIL is premature: a concrete retrieval/reconciliation gap remains plausible, the reference implements useful checks, and AP2 explicitly leaves practical retrieval outside its scope.

Narrow the hypothesis to: **For a developer already maintaining a consequential purchase across separate systems, TimeProofs reduces the recurring work of obtaining, reconciling and independently checking the required evidence compared with native verifiers and a small script.**

The single next experiment is the external authorized_purchase adoption test above. It must measure unassisted comprehension, actual sandbox integration, an independent consumer, repeat use and a concrete paid-operation commitment. No additional profiles, billing, dashboard, enterprise workflow or broad protocol expansion before its result. G6 ends here; the experiment is prepared, not launched automatically.
