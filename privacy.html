Privacy / data minimization

TimeProofs seals fingerprints, not sensitive AI content.

For AI Action Files, the company keeps the Action Files and original evidence. TimeProofs receives only fingerprints or payload hashes for sealing by default.

Company-owned storageHash-only by defaultLocal verification possiblePrivacy linter support
No absolute anonymity promise. If a company puts sensitive metadata inside an Action File, that metadata may remain sensitive even if TimeProofs only sees the hash. The company controls what it writes into the file.

Privacy model for Action Files

1

The company stores Action Files

Action Files are customer-owned records. They can be saved in Drive, SharePoint, S3, a CRM, an internal system, or a case folder.

2

TimeProofs receives hashes

For sealing, TimeProofs should receive payload_hash, format, action_id, proof level, and minimal non-sensitive metadata.

3

Verification can be local

The verifier can recompute the payload hash locally and check the Seal, so sensitive action data does not need to be uploaded for verification.

What TimeProofs should never receive by default

  • Raw prompts or full AI outputs.
  • Original documents, attachments, files, or uploaded datasets.
  • API keys, tokens, passwords, private keys, or credentials.
  • Personal data, health data, financial details, HR records, or confidential business content.
  • Large raw content fields when a fingerprint would be enough.
  • Full Action Files if only the canonical payload hash is needed for sealing.

What the company controls

Storage location

The company decides where Action Files and original evidence are stored. TimeProofs does not need to be the evidence repository by default.

Evidence policy

The company decides which fields are included, which fields are fingerprinted, and which business systems keep the original data.

Sharing

The company decides whether to share an Action File, a Seal, a hash, an incident pack, or only a verification result.

Retention

The company decides retention rules for Action Files in its own record system, subject to its internal policies and legal obligations.

How the privacy linter helps

The privacy linter is a safety layer. It warns when an Action File may contain sensitive or unnecessary raw data before the file is sealed, stored, or shared.

  • Warns on email-like or phone-like strings.
  • Warns on large raw content fields.
  • Warns when prompt or output text appears to be included instead of a fingerprint.
  • Recommends fingerprints or redacted summaries when raw content is not needed.
  • Does not guarantee that every sensitive value is detected.

Privacy limits

  • A hash can become linkable if the company or another party links it to an identified person, file, event, or system record.
  • TimeProofs cannot control what a company writes inside its own Action Files.
  • Local verification protects content from upload, but local devices and company storage still need normal security controls.
  • Proof levels do not replace legal review, regulated audit, DPIA work, or internal governance.
