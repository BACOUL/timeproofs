For companies

When your AI acts, you need to know what happened.

TimeProofs helps companies keep a signed, exportable, verifiable AI Action File for important AI-assisted actions in support, sales, procurement, operations, and risk workflows.

Customer-owned evidenceHash-only by defaultVerifier-friendlyPilot-ready
Company boundary. TimeProofs packages traceability for an AI action record. It does not judge whether the AI was correct, whether the action was legally valid, or whether a third party will accept the record.

Why companies need Action Files

Trust

AI is entering business workflows

Teams increasingly use AI to draft replies, summarize documents, update tools, prepare decisions, and trigger agentic tasks.

Traceability

Logs are not enough for business evidence

Logs help teams operate systems. An Action File gives a portable record that can be saved, shared, and verified later.

Control

Evidence should stay with the company

Companies can keep private evidence in their own systems while TimeProofs signs a fingerprint of the action record.

Who this is for

Support teams

Keep evidence that an AI assistant drafted, summarized, or prepared a ticket action.

Sales teams

Record AI-generated follow-ups, CRM summaries, and account actions without turning the CRM into a proof system.

Procurement teams

Attach an Action File to supplier reviews, RFQ analysis, or document summaries.

Operations teams

Package evidence around repeated agentic tasks and AI-assisted operational changes.

Risk teams

Review when AI was used in sensitive workflows while keeping the limits of proof explicit.

AI builders

Add a proof layer to AI automations without storing raw customer content in TimeProofs by default.

Start with a small pilot

The best first step is a narrow pilot around one repeated action. A support ticket, CRM follow-up, procurement summary, or agentic workflow update is enough to validate whether the Action File is useful.

Good pilot candidate

  • Repeated several times per week.
  • Clear business owner.
  • Easy to describe without raw sensitive content.
  • Useful if verified later by another person.

Bad pilot candidate

  • One-off experiment.
  • No buyer or owner.
  • Needs raw private data stored by default.
  • Requires a legal proof guarantee before any test.
