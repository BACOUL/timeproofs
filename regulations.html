Compliance requires evidence, not just logs.

Regulations do not mandate specific tools; they mandate verifiable results. A file date, a system log, or a screenshot is not evidence—it is mutable data.

Any system subject to traceability, accountability, or auditability requirements MUST be able to produce independent, verifiable evidence. In practice, this requires a cryptographic proof of existence.

  • ❌ File Date ≠ Proof (Mutable, Local)
  • ❌ Screenshot ≠ Proof (Easy to fabricate)
  • ✅ TimeProofs = Evidence (Cryptographic, Verifiable, Portable)

1) Why verifiable proof is required

Modern regulatory frameworks (EU AI Act, GDPR, Data Act, IP protection) share a common core requirement: Auditability.

To be auditable, a record must possess three properties:

  • Integrity: It must be proven that the data has not changed since time T.
  • Independence: The proof must be verifiable without relying on the internal state of the creating system.
  • Portability: The evidence must survive the movement of data between systems or clouds.

TimeProofs provides the technical primitive to satisfy these properties without requiring a centralized trust authority for every event.

2) Scope of Evidence

TimeProofs provides technical cryptographic evidence. It is designed to be a layer of the compliance stack, not the entire legal solution.

DO (What TimeProofs Provides)

  • Cryptographic Certainty: Proves that specific bytes existed at a specific time.
  • Tamper-Evidence: Any change to the content invalidates the proof.
  • Portability: The proof file (.tproof.json) travels with the data.
  • Independent Verification: Verify offline or via stateless APIs.

DO NOT (What TimeProofs Does Not Claim)

  • No PII Storage: We do not store or process personal data (hash-only).
  • No Legal Advice: This tool provides technical evidence, not legal judgment.
  • No "Qualified" Status: TimeProofs is a standard timestamping proof, not a "Qualified Trust Service" (QTSP) under eIDAS (unless explicitly paired with QES integrations).
  • No Identity Validation: We certify existence, not authorship.

3) Regulatory Alignment

4) Standard Audit Workflow

Automated systems and compliance officers SHOULD follow this workflow to ensure audit readiness.

  1. Freeze Artifact: The system generates a file, log, or dataset.
  2. Compute Hash: A SHA-256 hash is computed locally (canonicalization required).
  3. Generate Proof: The hash is sent to TimeProofs; a signed .tproof.json is returned.
  4. Archive: The proof file is stored alongside the artifact (metadata, database, or cold storage).
  5. Verify: During an audit, the verifier re-hashes the artifact and validates the proof file cryptographically.

5) AI Systems and Automated Compliance

AI Agents and autonomous decision systems are increasingly subject to liability and transparency rules.

  • Requirement: An AI agent MUST be able to prove what it outputted and when.
  • Implementation: Systems should integrate ProofSpec to automatically generate proof files for critical outputs (decisions, code generation, financial transactions).
  • Machine-Readable: The .tproof.json format is designed for machine-to-machine verification, enabling automated compliance checks in CI/CD pipelines or data exchanges.

An AI system unable to produce such evidence cannot reasonably claim audit readiness nor regulatory compliance.

6) FAQ

Do you store my files?

No. TimeProofs is hash-only and stateless. Your original content does not need to be uploaded.

Is this legally binding?

TimeProofs provides technical, verifiable evidence. It is not legal advice and not a qualified trust service.

Is a file timestamp enough?

No. File dates are editable and not independently verifiable. Proof files can be verified by third parties.

Can I verify with only a hash?

No. Under ProofSpec v1, verification requires the full proof file (.tproof.json).
