Regulations & Compliance

Hash-only, stateless, open verification. This page maps how TimeProofs aligns with the EU AI Act, GDPR, Data Act, and other frameworks.

Overview

Model. Hash on client. Timestamp via API. Verify publicly. No content upload. No profiling.

Versions. v0.1 provides the signed timestamp service. v0.2 adds a stateless layer of portable proofs via .tproof.json receipts, while keeping the hash-only posture.

Implication. A minimal-footprint trust utility that supports audit trails and traceability, aligned with core privacy principles: minimization, purpose limitation, integrity, transparency.

TimeProofs aligns with the spirit of the EU AI Act, Data Act, and GDPR by staying stateless, hash-only, and providing open verification plus portable .tproof.json envelopes.

New in v0.2

  • Stateless API posture — no stored originals, no content logs.
  • .tproof.json receipts for portable, verifiable proof envelopes under your control.
  • Offline verification paths (browser and CLI) based on the same proof format.
  • Updated mapping across EU and global frameworks with a hash-only design.

EU framework

RegulationRelevanceTimeProofs stance
EU AI Act Classification, transparency Infrastructure utility. No profiling. Public verification and .tproof.json receipts support provenance, traceability, and technical documentation for AI/data pipelines.
GDPR Data protection Hash-only by design. Controllers should avoid embedding personal data in optional meta fields, including inside .tproof.json receipts.
Data Act Portability, cloud neutrality .tproof.json receipts can be stored locally or in any cloud. The protocol is provider-neutral and reduces lock-in by design.
eIDAS2 / QEAA Qualified trust services Protocol-agnostic. Optional bridges to qualified services are possible in the future; current proofs are non-qualified cryptographic timestamp evidence.
DSA / DMA Platform obligations B2B utility, not a consumer platform. Transparency comes from open verification, open specification, and portable receipts for audit trails.

Controllers remain responsible for legal basis and downstream data handling. Do not upload originals to TimeProofs.

Data minimization

  • No uploads. Only 64-hex SHA-256 hashes are sent to the API.
  • Metadata is optional. Keep hints generic. Avoid personal data and secrets.
  • Minimal records. Proofs store hash, timestamp, signature, and small optional metadata.
  • .tproof.json under your control. Receipts can be stored entirely by the organization without changing the hash-only model.

Audit & evidence

Each proof provides enough information to be used as cryptographic evidence in audit workflows.

{
  "ok": true,
  "version": "v0.2",
  "hash": "7c040f...d76c2",
  "ts": "2025-10-22T12:34:56.789Z",
  "sig": "<signature>",
  "alg": "HMAC-SHA256|Ed25519",
  "issuer": "timeproofs.io",
  "verify_url": "https://api.timeproofs.io/api/verify?hash=...",
  "receipt": "https://example.org/proofs/file-123.tproof.json"
}

Store the verify_url plus the receipt reference alongside your internal logs to prove integrity over time.
